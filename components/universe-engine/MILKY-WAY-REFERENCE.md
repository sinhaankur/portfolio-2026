# Milky Way hero — reference spec

**The target is the naked-eye Milky Way BAND seen from inside the galaxy**
(looking toward the galactic centre — Sagittarius / Scorpius / Rho Ophiuchi),
NOT a face-on spiral galaxy. Confirmed by the user with four astrophotography
references (long-exposure, photoreal). Fidelity target: **as photoreal as
possible**. The user's phrase: **"gases and clouds and elements"** — render the
colourful *interstellar medium*, not just a field of star dots.

## What must be in it (from the references)

1. **Luminous star-cloud band** — billions of unresolved stars forming soft
   glowing clouds. Brightest + warmest (tan/gold) toward the galactic centre,
   cooler grey-blue at the edges. A diagonal band, soft and cloudy.
2. **Dark dust lanes — the Great Rift** — dramatic BLACK rifts and tendrils
   cutting *through* the bright clouds. This is the single most defining feature:
   the dark structure matters as much as the light. Carved hard, filamentary.
3. **Blue reflection nebulae** — dust lit blue by nearby hot stars (the Rho Oph
   blue clouds). Soft blue glows around bright blue stars.
4. **Pink / magenta Hα emission nebulae** — ionised hydrogen (Lagoon, Trifid,
   Antares region). Small bright pink-magenta knots along the band.
5. **Gold / amber emission** — dust glowing warm around bright stars (the Rho Oph
   golden clouds around Antares).
6. **Bright foreground stars** — a few large stars with subtle diffraction spikes,
   over a dense starfield of varied colour-temperature dots.
7. **Globular clusters** — tight bright star-balls (e.g. M4 by Antares).

## Do NOT
- Do not build a face-on logarithmic spiral with spinning arms — that's the wrong
  reference. (Tried + reverted 2026-11-03.)
- Do not rely on the star-point field alone — dots read as "scattered dots". The
  continuous light (haze/nebula/dust shaders) must carry the clouds + gas; stars
  sparkle on top.
